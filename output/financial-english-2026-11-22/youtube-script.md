An economist's capital and a farmer's cattle are the same word. | 拉丁語 *caput* 是「頭」，中世紀拉丁語 *capitale* 是「財產」。英文從它得到 *capital*、*cattle*、*chattel*。經濟學家的 capital 和農夫的 cattle 是同一個字。 |

| 1:08 | 羅馬神廟立在山丘上，旁邊有人在鑄幣 | Next, money moved into a temple. Rome struck its coins near the temple of Juno Moneta. *Moneta* came to mean a mint, and it gave English *money*. | 接著，錢搬進了神廟。羅馬在 Juno Moneta 的神廟附近鑄幣。*Moneta* 後來指鑄幣廠，也給了英文 *money*。 |

| 1:30 | 海貝 → 甲骨文「貝」→ 財、貨、買、賣、貴依序浮出 | In China, money came from the sea. The character 貝 is a cowrie shell, which is why 財, 貨, 買, 賣 and 貴 all contain it. | 在中國，錢來自海裡。「貝」是海貝的象形字，所以財、貨、買、賣、貴都帶著它。 |
| 1:41 | 文藝復興風格的錢商坐在長凳後面 | Then money sat down. *Bank* comes from Italian *banca*, a bench: the counter where money-dealers worked. | 然後，錢坐了下來。*Bank* 來自義大利語 *banca*，長凳，也就是錢商工作的檯面。 |
| 1:50 | 長凳裂成兩半，畫面打上「存疑」印章 | *Bankrupt* is *banca rotta*, a broken bench. You may hear that failed dealers had their benches smashed. Etymologists doubt it. It was probably just a figure of speech. | *Bankrupt* 是 *banca rotta*，破掉的長凳。你可能聽過：倒閉的錢商會被砸凳子。詞源學家對此存疑，那大概只是比喻。 |
| 2:04 | 一顆心放上天平 | Finally, money became a promise. *Credit* comes from Latin *credere*, to believe. Literally: to put your heart on it. | 最後，錢變成了承諾。*Credit* 來自拉丁語 *credere*，相信。字面意思是：把心放上去。 |
| 2:14 | 「信」拆成「人」＋「言」 | Chinese says 信用. 信 is a person and their word. Latin bets the heart; Chinese bets the word. | 中文說「信用」。「信」是人加上言。拉丁語押上心，中文押上話。 |
| 2:23 | 股市圖表急跌，信用卡褪色 | So when credit dries up in a crisis, the word tells you exactly what happened: people stopped believing. | 所以危機中信用枯竭時，這個字已經告訴你發生了什麼：大家不再相信了。 |
| 2:33 | 字卡 *dette* → 一個 b 被夾子夾進去 → *debt* | And *debt*? It used to be spelled *dette*. Scholars later added a *b* to match Latin *debitum*. Nobody ever said it. | 那 *debt* 呢？以前拼成 *dette*。後來學者為了對應拉丁語 *debitum* 加了一個 *b*，但從來沒有人念出來。 |
