# The Word That Moves
# 會搬家的字

> **A metaphor is a moving truck for meaning: it carries a word from one place to another. See that, and a whole family of English words opens up.**
>
> **Metaphor 是一輛搬運意義的車：把一個字從原本的位置搬到新的位置。看懂這一點，一整串英文字跟著解鎖。**

## 1. Hiring a "Metaphoric" Company｜請一家「隱喻公司」來搬家

If you move house in Athens, you will probably hire a μεταφορική εταιρεία (metaforikí etairía), which simply means "a moving company." Read the first word slowly and you will recognize it: *metaphoric*. In Modern Greek, μεταφορά (metaphorá) is still an everyday word for transport. The word that English saves for poetry class is the word Greeks use for boxes, sofas and delivery vans.

如果你在雅典搬家，大概會找一家 μεταφορική εταιρεία（metaforikí etairía），意思就是「搬家公司」。把第一個字慢慢念出來，你會認得它：*metaphoric*。在現代希臘語裡，μεταφορά（metaphorá）至今仍是「運輸、搬運」的日常用字。英文留給文學課的這個字，希臘人拿來講紙箱、沙發和貨車。

The French thinker Michel de Certeau noticed the same thing in his 1980 book on everyday life. In Athens, he observed, public buses and trains are called *metaphorai* — so going to work means riding a metaphor.

法國思想家 Michel de Certeau 在 1980 年討論日常生活的著作裡也注意到這件事。他寫道，在雅典，公車和火車叫做 *metaphorai*——所以搭車上班，就是搭一個「metaphor」。

This is not a pun. It is the original meaning. Before metaphor was a figure of speech, it was a physical action: carrying something from one place to another.

這不是雙關語，而是這個字的本義。Metaphor 在成為修辭手法之前，先是一個身體動作：把東西從一個地方搬到另一個地方。

## 2. Taking the Word Apart｜把這個字拆開

Metaphor comes from the Greek *metaphora*, "a transfer" or "a carrying over." It has two parts: *meta-*, meaning "across" or "over," and *pherein*, meaning "to carry" or "to bear." The related verb *metapherein* meant to move something, to change it — and, already in ancient Greek, to use a word in an unusual sense.

Metaphor 來自希臘語 *metaphora*，意思是「轉移」或「搬過去」。它由兩個部分組成：*meta-* 是「跨越、越過」，*pherein* 是「攜帶、承載」。相關動詞 *metapherein* 的意思是搬動某物、改變某物——而早在古希臘語裡，它就已經有「把一個字用在不尋常的意思上」這個用法。

`meta-`（跨越）＋ `pherein`（攜帶）→ `metaphora`（搬過去、轉移）

In other words, when the Greeks needed a word for this trick of language, they borrowed one from the world of moving objects. The word *metaphor* is itself a metaphor.

換句話說，希臘人需要一個字來稱呼這種語言技巧時，是從「搬東西」的世界借來的。*Metaphor* 這個字本身，就是一個 metaphor。

From Greek the word passed into Latin as *metaphora*, into Old French as *metafore* in the 13th century, and finally into English in the late 1400s.

這個字從希臘語進入拉丁語成為 *metaphora*，13 世紀進入古法語成為 *metafore*，15 世紀後期終於進入英語。

One warning about *meta-*. Today people say a joke is "very meta" when it talks about itself, or that a discussion happens "at a meta level," one level higher. That modern sense came much later, from a misreading of *metaphysics*, and it produced words like *metalanguage* (1936). In *metaphor*, *meta-* has nothing to do with "higher" or "about itself." It means across — from here to there.

關於 *meta-*，要提醒一件事。現在大家說一個笑話「很 meta」，是指它在講自己；說某個討論在「meta 層次」，是指更高一層。這個現代意思出現得晚得多，來自對 *metaphysics*（形上學）的誤讀，也因此產生了 *metalanguage*（後設語言，1936）這類字。但在 *metaphor* 裡，*meta-* 跟「更高」或「自我指涉」無關，它的意思是「跨越」——從這裡到那裡。

## 3. The Latin Twin｜拉丁雙胞胎

The Romans built their own word for the same idea, using Latin parts with exactly the same meaning. *Trans-* means "across," and *ferre* means "to carry." Together, *transferre* means "to carry across" — the source of English *transfer*. Its past participle was *translatus*, and the related noun was *translatio*.

羅馬人用拉丁語的零件，為同一個概念造了自己的字，而且零件的意思一模一樣。*Trans-* 是「跨越」，*ferre* 是「攜帶」。合起來的 *transferre* 就是「搬過去」——英文 *transfer* 的來源。它的過去分詞是 *translatus*，相關名詞是 *translatio*。

Why does *translatus* look so different from *ferre*? Because the Latin verb for "carry" was irregular. Its past participle, *lātus*, was borrowed from a different verb altogether — much as English *went* has nothing to do with *go*.

為什麼 *translatus* 跟 *ferre* 長得差那麼多？因為拉丁語的「攜帶」是不規則動詞，它的過去分詞 *lātus* 是從另一個動詞借來的——就像英文的 *went* 跟 *go* 在字形上毫無關係一樣。

Around AD 95, the Roman rhetoric teacher Quintilian made the connection explicit. In his textbook on public speaking, he introduced metaphor as "the Greek term for our *translatio*" (tr. H. E. Butler, 1920).

大約西元 95 年，羅馬修辭學老師 Quintilian 把這層關係說得很明白。他在演說教科書裡介紹 metaphor 時寫道：這是「我們拉丁語 *translatio* 的希臘說法」。

From *translatus*, English later took the word *translate*. So *metaphor* and *translate* are twins: one Greek, one Latin, both meaning "carry across." For bilingual learners, this is worth a pause. When you translate a sentence from Chinese into English, you are doing what a metaphor does — carrying meaning over a border, and accepting that something changes on the way.

英文後來從 *translatus* 得到了 *translate*。所以 *metaphor* 和 *translate* 是一對雙胞胎：一個希臘、一個拉丁，意思都是「搬過去」。對雙語學習者來說，這一點值得停下來想一想。當你把一句中文翻成英文，你做的正是 metaphor 在做的事——把意思搬過一道邊界，並且接受它在路上會有一點改變。

## 4. One Root, Three Roads｜一個詞根，三條路

Behind *pherein* and *ferre* stands an older root, which linguists reconstruct as \*bher-, "to carry" (and also "to bear children"). From that single root, words reached English by three different roads.

*pherein* 和 *ferre* 背後，還有一個更古老的詞根，語言學家把它重建為 \*bher-，意思是「攜帶」（也有「生育」之意）。從這一個詞根出發，字沿著三條不同的路來到英語。

**The Greek road** gives us words with *-pher-* or *-phor-*. *Euphoria* comes from "bearing well." *Phosphorus* means "light-bringer." An *amphora* is a jar carried by both sides, thanks to its two handles. The *periphery* is the line carried around a circle. A *semaphore* is a "signal-bearer."

**希臘路線**給了我們帶 *-pher-* 或 *-phor-* 的字。*Euphoria*（狂喜）來自「承受得好」。*Phosphorus*（磷）的意思是「帶來光的」。*Amphora*（雙耳瓶）是兩邊都能提的瓶子，因為它有兩個把手。*Periphery*（周邊）是繞著圓周「搬一圈」的那條線。*Semaphore*（旗語、號誌）是「攜帶信號的東西」。

**The Latin road** gives us *-fer*: *transfer* (carry across), *refer* (carry back), *offer* (carry toward someone), *prefer* (carry in front, so put first), *differ* (carry apart), *suffer* (carry from underneath, so endure), and *fertile* (able to bear fruit).

**拉丁路線**給了我們 *-fer*：*transfer*（搬過去）、*refer*（帶回去）、*offer*（帶到某人面前）、*prefer*（帶到前面，也就是放第一位）、*differ*（分開帶）、*suffer*（在底下扛著，也就是承受）、*fertile*（能結果實的）。

**The native road** skipped Greek and Latin entirely. *Bear*, *birth* and *burden* all come from the same root through Old English. A burden is, quite literally, "what is carried."

**本土路線**完全沒經過希臘或拉丁。*Bear*（承受；生育）、*birth*（出生）、*burden*（負擔）都經由古英語來自同一個詞根。Burden 的字面意思就是「被扛著的東西」。

Now a trap. *Ferry* looks like *ferre*, and a ferry carries people across water. But etymologists trace it to a different root, \*per-, "to lead, pass over." Similar sound and similar meaning do not prove a family connection; only the documented history does. That is a useful habit for any word learner.

接著是一個陷阱。*Ferry*（渡輪）長得像 *ferre*，渡輪也確實載人過河。但詞源學家把它追溯到另一個詞根 \*per-，意思是「引導、越過」。發音像、意思也像，都不能證明是同一家族；只有有文獻可查的演變史才能。這是每個學單字的人都該養成的習慣。

## 5. The Lion Test｜獅子測驗

Quintilian also gave one of the clearest tests for telling a metaphor from a simile. If you say a man did something like a lion, he explained, that is a comparison; if you say "He is a lion," that is a metaphor.

Quintilian 也提供了區分 metaphor 和 simile 最清楚的測驗之一。他解釋：說一個人「像獅子一樣」做某件事，是比較；說「他是一頭獅子」，才是 metaphor。

In English grammar terms: *She fought like a lion* is a **simile**, because *like* or *as* openly marks the comparison. *She is a lion in negotiations* is a **metaphor**: the word *lion* has been carried into a new place and set down with no label.

用英文文法的說法：*She fought like a lion* 是 **simile（明喻）**，因為 *like* 或 *as* 明白標出了比較。*She is a lion in negotiations* 是 **metaphor（隱喻、暗喻）**：*lion* 這個字被搬到新的位置，直接放下，沒有貼任何標籤。

Why does the difference matter? A simile keeps the two ideas side by side and tells you they are similar. A metaphor does the carrying for you and leaves you to unpack it. That is why metaphors feel stronger, and also why they are harder for learners: the comparison is hidden, so you first have to notice that something has been moved.

這個差別為什麼重要？Simile 把兩個概念並排擺著，告訴你它們很像。Metaphor 則直接幫你搬好，留給你自己拆箱。這就是為什麼 metaphor 讀起來更有力，也是為什麼它對學習者更難：比較被藏起來了，你得先察覺「有東西被搬過」。

Four centuries before Quintilian, Aristotle defined metaphor as "giving the thing a name that belongs to something else" (tr. Ingram Bywater). He also considered it the hardest skill to teach: "it is the mark of genius, for to make good metaphors implies an eye for resemblances" (tr. S. H. Butcher, 1895).

比 Quintilian 早四個世紀，Aristotle 把 metaphor 定義為「把屬於另一件事物的名稱，給予這件事物」。他也認為這是最難傳授的能力：這是天才的標誌，因為要造出好的 metaphor，必須有一雙看出相似之處的眼睛。

## 6. From the Battlefield to the Dinner Table｜從戰場到餐桌

For centuries, metaphor was treated as decoration, something poets add to make language beautiful. Quintilian already doubted this: he noted that metaphor is so natural that people use it without noticing, even people with no education at all. In 1980, the linguist George Lakoff and the philosopher Mark Johnson pushed the idea much further in *Metaphors We Live By*. They argued that metaphor is not only how we decorate language but how we organize thought, an idea now known as **conceptual metaphor**.

好幾個世紀以來，metaphor 被當成裝飾品，是詩人用來美化語言的東西。Quintilian 早就不這麼認為：他指出 metaphor 自然到人們用了都沒察覺，連完全沒受過教育的人也在用。1980 年，語言學家 George Lakoff 和哲學家 Mark Johnson 在《Metaphors We Live By》中把這個想法推得更遠。他們主張，metaphor 不只是我們修飾語言的方式，更是我們組織思考的方式，這個觀點現在稱為 **conceptual metaphor（概念隱喻）**。

Their best-known example is ARGUMENT IS WAR. Listen to how English talks about a disagreement: we *defend* a position, *attack* a weak point, *shoot down* an idea, and *win* or *lose* an argument. Nobody is fighting, yet the whole vocabulary comes from the battlefield. The meaning was carried over long ago and left there so long that we forget it was ever moved.

他們最有名的例子是 ARGUMENT IS WAR（辯論是戰爭）。聽聽英文怎麼講意見不合：我們 *defend*（防守）一個立場、*attack*（攻擊）弱點、*shoot down*（擊落）一個想法、*win* 或 *lose*（贏或輸）一場辯論。沒有人真的在打仗，但整套詞彙都來自戰場。這些意思很久以前就被搬了過來，放得太久，久到我們忘了它們曾經被搬過。

Chinese does exactly the same thing. A heated debate is 唇槍舌劍 — lips like spears, tongues like swords. An online flame war in Taiwan is a 筆戰, a "pen war." Two unrelated languages arrived at the same picture, which suggests the metaphor says something about human thinking, not just about English.

中文也做了一模一樣的事。激烈的辯論叫「唇槍舌劍」——嘴唇是槍、舌頭是劍。在台灣，網路上吵起來叫「筆戰」。兩個毫無親緣關係的語言，走到了同一個畫面，這表示這個隱喻反映的是人類的思考方式，而不只是英文的習慣。

Some metaphors have been carried so far that they no longer feel like metaphors at all. A mountain has a *foot*, a table has *legs*, and a narrow road has a *bottleneck*. Chinese says 山腳, 桌腳 and 瓶頸, almost word for word. Linguists call these **dead metaphors**: the truck has been unloaded, and the furniture now lives in its new home.

有些隱喻被搬得太遠，遠到已經感覺不出是隱喻了。山有 *foot*（山腳），桌子有 *legs*（桌腳），狹窄的路段有 *bottleneck*（瓶頸）。中文的說法幾乎逐字對應。語言學家稱之為 **dead metaphor（死隱喻）**：卡車已經卸完貨，家具在新家住下來了。

## 7. How to Use This｜怎麼用在學習上

The practical lesson is simple. When an English word feels abstract, ask what physical picture was carried into it. To *grasp* an idea is to hold it in your hand. A *bottleneck* is a narrow neck that slows everything down. An *indefensible* claim is one that cannot be protected, like a fort with no walls. And when you meet *-fer* or *-pher*, look for the carrying. One root, one picture, a dozen words. That is what a good metaphor does for meaning, and what a good etymology does for memory.

實際的用法很簡單。遇到一個感覺很抽象的英文字，問問自己：是哪個具體畫面被搬進了這個字？*Grasp* 一個想法，就是把它握在手裡。*Bottleneck* 是一個讓一切慢下來的窄瓶頸。*Indefensible* 的論點，就是守不住的論點，像一座沒有城牆的堡壘。遇到 *-fer* 或 *-pher*，就去找「攜帶」的意象。一個詞根、一個畫面、一打單字。好的 metaphor 為意義做的事，就是好的詞源為記憶做的事。

Try it with three words from this lesson. Why does *prefer* mean "like better"? Because you carry the thing you like to the front. Why does *suffer* mean "endure"? Because you are underneath, carrying the weight. Why does a *burden* feel heavy? Because it is, by definition, something carried. Once you can see the truck, you no longer have to memorize the cargo.

用這一課的三個字試試看。*Prefer* 為什麼是「比較喜歡」？因為你把喜歡的東西搬到最前面。*Suffer* 為什麼是「承受」？因為你在底下扛著重量。*Burden* 為什麼沉重？因為它的定義就是「被扛著的東西」。只要看得見那輛卡車，你就不必死背車上的貨。

## Quick Check｜小測驗

1. In *metaphor*, what does *meta-* mean? ｜ *metaphor* 裡的 *meta-* 是什麼意思？
   (a) higher 更高 (b) about itself 自我指涉 (c) across 跨越
2. Which word is the Latin twin of *metaphor*? ｜ 哪個字是 *metaphor* 的拉丁雙胞胎？
   (a) translate (b) transmit (c) transform
3. Which word is **not** from the \*bher- family? ｜ 哪個字**不屬於** \*bher- 家族？
   (a) suffer (b) ferry (c) euphoria
4. Which sentence is a metaphor? ｜ 哪一句是 metaphor？
   (a) He runs like the wind. (b) He is a walking dictionary. (c) She sings as sweetly as a bird.

### Answers｜解答

1. **(c)** The "higher" sense of *meta* came later, from a misreading of *metaphysics*. ｜「更高」的意思是後來對 *metaphysics* 的誤讀。
2. **(a)** *translate* comes from *translatus*, the past participle of *transferre*, "carry across." ｜ *translate* 來自 *transferre*（搬過去）的過去分詞 *translatus*。
3. **(b)** *ferry* comes from a different root, \*per-, "to lead, pass over." ｜ *ferry* 來自另一個詞根 \*per-（引導、越過）。
4. **(b)** (a) and (c) use *like* and *as*, so they are similes. ｜ (a)(c) 用了 *like*、*as*，是 simile（明喻）。
